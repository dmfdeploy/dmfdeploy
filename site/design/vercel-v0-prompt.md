```text
Design a distinctive, responsive two-page website for dmfdeploy.io: Home (/) and Become a founding sponsor (/sponsor). Produce desktop and mobile views for both pages, with reusable visual tokens and components. This is a public open-source engineering initiative, not a commercial SaaS product.

Project and audience
dmfdeploy is a community-governed, vendor-neutral effort to build shared infrastructure for software-defined broadcast and media facilities. Its broad vision is “A shared foundation for dynamic media.” Manufacturers, broadcasters, systems integrators, engineering collaborators, and potential founding sponsors need to understand the purpose, inspect the technical path, see what has actually been demonstrated, and know how to participate. The EBU Dynamic Media Facility reference architecture informs the vocabulary; dmfdeploy is independent and has no implied EBU endorsement.

Creative direction
Make an editorial, premium industrial design with confident typography, generous composition, precise diagrams, and a continuous dark canvas. Retain the supplied project logo and its established warm magenta/pink-to-orange gradient as a signature field near the home hero, then let it recede. The real 006 film still should appear dark and legible against that field; do not put another gradient over its thumbnail. Avoid generic SaaS cards, repeated boxed horizontal sections, teal accent stripes at card tops, fake dashboards, stock photography, and decorative shapes. The logo's ellipse geometry may inform connected flow and grouping only where it explains a relationship. Avoid free-floating orbits and pointless ellipses. Let sections transition naturally as the visitor scrolls. Use restrained, purposeful motion, such as a connector drawing along the operations path or the video enlarging after a click. All content must remain visible without animation; support reduced motion. If supplied facility photos are used, crop or darken them so no sensitive detail is legible.

Home page
- Hero headline: “A shared foundation for dynamic media.” Add one short sentence explaining an open foundation for declaring intent, deploying workloads, and observing media facilities. Pair it with the actual 006 pitch film: real supplied poster, clear play affordance, user-initiated playback, no sound autoplay. Include useful links to the architecture and the public thesis.
- Explain the industry problem: media functions are moving to software, IP, and commodity compute while deployment, inventory, identity, secrets, and visibility are often fragmented. Present the project vision without reducing it to today's prototype scope.
- Make the architecture the signature visual, based on the five-part operations loop shown in film 006: Operations, Automation, Facility, Observability, and Source of truth at the center. Show directional relationships and a dotted return or planned link where appropriate. Create a separately composed mobile diagram; do not merely shrink a desktop SVG. The diagram must distinguish demonstrated steps from the fuller intended loop.
- Show standards and tools accurately: EBU DMF as reference vocabulary; k3s, AWX, Helm, NetBox, and Prometheus in the current deployment/observation path; AMWA NMOS IS-05 as planned v0.2 work; Media eXchange Layer as an active experiment, not a production media plane.
- Give project status a clear evidence-led treatment. First-party demonstrated: a single-node ARM64 prototype with catalog intent through AWX/k3s/Helm execution, NetBox lifecycle, Prometheus targets, and an operator console. Still open: independent cold-start reproduction, team operation and IS-05 switching, facility planning. Do not claim production ST 2110/PTP, multi-node high availability, cross-site operation, benchmark results, or a finished media plant.
- Explain open participation: public discussion, design records, reviewed contributions, Apache-2.0 code. Link to GitHub. Include one clear sponsorship CTA linking to /sponsor, plus GitHub (https://github.com/dmfdeploy) and YouTube (https://www.youtube.com/@dmfdeploy). Do not create placeholder social buttons.

Sponsor page
- Title: “Become a founding sponsor.” Make this a practical, conversion-oriented funding brief, visually related to Home.
- Explain the strategic value separately for manufacturers, broadcasters, and integrators: open integration points, a reviewable operational path, and shared deployment experience. Do not promise commercial advantage or privileged roadmap control.
- Show proposed funding priorities: v0.1 independent reproduction and documentation; v0.2 team console, nonblocking deployment, and NMOS IS-05 switching; v0.3 workload/facility planning; continuing public documentation and validation. These are proposals, with no invented delivery dates or guaranteed outcomes.
- Present four clearly labeled proposed annual levels: €1,000, €5,000, €15,000, and €25,000+. Recognition terms are not confirmed. Make the amounts easy to compare without turning the page into a pricing grid for a product.
- Explain current community-led technical decisions through public discussion, RFCs, architecture records, maintainer review, and DCO sign-off. A founder-protected mission charter is proposed and unratified. Sponsorship would not buy votes, vetoes, feature priority, or technical control.
- Financial arrangements: fiscal host unconfirmed, Open Collective reporting proposed and dependent on setup, corporate invoicing conditional on the eventual host. There is no active payment link. Show this status plainly before the inquiry action. The only live conversion mechanism is mailto:inquiries@dmfdeploy.io for corporate inquiries; never invent a checkout, payment button, sponsor logos, endorsements, or operating fiscal host.

Implementation constraints
The production site uses Astro, Tailwind CSS, TypeScript, minimal client-side JavaScript, and static Cloudflare Pages deployment. Make the design portable to semantic Astro components. Avoid Next.js-only behavior, heavy component libraries, and animation dependencies. If your generation environment requires React, treat it as a visual prototype and keep markup/styles easy to port. Provide a concise component/token specification and a short list of any content that still requires verification. Meet accessible contrast, keyboard focus, responsive typography, and sensible touch targets. Prefer concise, specific copy over marketing filler. Do not invent sponsors, endorsements, milestones, performance claims, or operational capabilities.
```
